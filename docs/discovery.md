# Discovery provenance and release boundaries

Available source: user release brief and recovered original conversation user messages for Rounds 3, 4 and 5. The retrieval returned five turns, no older cursor, and opaque placeholders for previous assistant answers. Rounds 1–2 and the original ten-version roadmap were not accessible. This release does not claim to reproduce them; the roadmap here is a provisional continuation of the explicit release scope.

Verified from the release brief: 80–119 enrolled students; 40–69 busy-day visits; two staff iPads; two MacBooks; two main adults; 5–6 early learners sometimes need simultaneous support; more than 75% paper use; flexible arrivals. No existing scanner was confirmed.

Round 3: homework preparation normally 31–60 minutes, sometimes 1–2 hours during class; homework in named student pouches; handoff typically 1–2 minutes; grading usually 1–2 hours, sometimes longer; the second adult mostly grades; an additional adult assists two days weekly. Physical record books are nominally updated at departure but can lag to hours later or the next day. KUMON CONNECT reduces workload. Corrections are marked on worksheets; incomplete work is tracked manually and inconsistently. No automatic grading is proposed.

Round 4: required corporate/regional reporting is entered in Kumon's online portal; 80–119 records reviewed monthly; 3–5 hours preparing the report; physical record books are the main source; usage is individual completed worksheet sheets; CONNECT provides counts for some students. Exact fields, required deadline date, report export format, and frequency of organizing records remain unverified. Parent progress reporting is desired but no verified reporting template is available.

Round 5: Wi-Fi is reported very reliable; reception/entrance is the natural check-in location; pouches have handwritten names; no consistent in-center attendance tracking; quick choices plus optional voice notes preferred; 21–30 seconds available per observation; pickup/drop-off conversations are common. Electronic attendance is required with provider flexibility, but the actual required event fields, retention, and compliance workflow are unverified. All authorized staff need differentiated access. Preferred arrival windows can be encouraged, without reservations. The primary desired improvement is individual attention for early learners. Corporate/privacy storage requirements still need verification.

Likely bottlenecks ranked: early-learner attention; grading/corrections competing with instruction; record-book backlog; homework preparation/handoff interruption; missing occupancy visibility; monthly reporting re-entry. This app reduces tracking friction and forgotten follow-ups; it cannot create staff capacity or validate learning outcomes. Quick observations are supplemental to official records, not a replacement for record books or Kumon systems.

Uncertainties: actual center plan and station capacity, staff role assignments and kiosk supervision, duration and concurrency of visits, exact worksheet counting rules, source-of-truth decisions, check-in compliance, parent consent/authorization, privacy retention/access/deletion, voice-provider processing, device/browser versions, and corporate integration permission. Time ranges with longer additional notes are preserved as variability, not reconciled into invented averages.
